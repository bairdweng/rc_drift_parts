# TRX-4 Magnetic Light Connector — Easy Body Disconnect for LED Upgrades

## If you have lights on your TRX-4, this little problem gets annoying fast

Taking the body off your TRX-4 isn't difficult.

But once you add **LED lights, light bars, or other electronics to the body**, there's one annoying extra step:

**You have to unplug the wires every time you remove the body.**

Do it once? No big deal.

Do it every time you change the battery, clean the truck, or work on the chassis?

It gets old pretty quickly.

The wires usually run from your **receiver or light module on the chassis** up to **lights mounted on the body, bumper, or roof**. That means the plug lives somewhere tight — near the receiver box, behind the grille, or buried between body posts. Every body-off moment turns into a small wiring project.

## A simple solution: a 3-pin magnetic quick disconnect

A small **3-pin magnetic connector** can be installed between the body and chassis.

When you put the body back on:

**Bring the two connectors close → they snap together.**

When you need to remove the body:

**Pull the body away → the connection separates.**

No more reaching inside the truck to unplug the connector every time.

### What you're actually buying

This is a **3-pin servo-style cable** — not a battery power lead:

| End            | What it is                                   | What it connects to                                |
| -------------- | -------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------- |
| Magnetic side  | Quick-disconnect pair (you need both halves) | Chassis-side mate wired to receiver or light port  |
| JR female side | Standard 3-pin servo socket                  | JR male plug from your light kit or LED controller |

The three wires are the same as any RC servo lead: **positive (+), negative (−), and signal**. If your light kit already uses a JR plug, this drops in without any soldering.

**Important:** Magnetic connectors work in **pairs**. One half mounts on the chassis (wired to your receiver AUX / light port). The other half is on this cable, running toward your lights. Confirm your kit includes both halves before you install.

## Where to put it on a TRX-4

The whole point is placement. Put the magnetic joint **right at the body/chassis split** — not down by the suspension where links flex and pull on the wires.

A layout that works on most TRX-4 builds:

```text
Receiver (AUX / light port)
        ↓
Chassis-side magnetic half  ← zip-tied to receiver box or frame rail
        ↕  snap
Magnetic half on this cable
        ↓
JR female → light kit plug
        ↓
LED bar / rock lights on body
```

**Routing tips:**

- Secure the **chassis half** so it doesn't move when you disconnect — a zip tie on the receiver box plate works well.
- Run the **body-side wire** along the inner grille or windshield post. Keep it away from tire and suspension travel.
- Leave a little slack on the body side so normal flex doesn't pull the magnets apart.
- If you also run [magnetic body mounts](/guides/cnc-magnetic-body-mount-guide), this pairs naturally — clip-free body removal plus clip-free light wiring.

## How to install it (5 steps)

### Step 1 — Find your light port

On most Traxxas TRX-4 setups, lights connect through the **light control module** or an **AUX channel** on the TQi receiver. Check your light kit manual for the correct port before wiring.

### Step 2 — Wire the chassis-side magnetic half

Unplug your light kit from the receiver. Connect the **chassis-side magnetic mate** to the receiver or light module. Fix it to the chassis with a zip tie or tape so it stays put.

### Step 3 — Plug in your light kit

Connect your light kit's **JR male plug** into the **JR female end** of this cable. Make sure the plug is fully seated — tab aligned, no bent pins.

### Step 4 — Snap the magnets together

Align the two halves flat. They should click together with a firm snap. Give a gentle tug to confirm the hold.

### Step 5 — Test before you button everything up

Power on. Toggle your lights on and off. Disconnect the magnets, reconnect, and test again. Then remove the body fully and check that no wires catch on tires, links, or driveshafts.

## Is it worth adding to your TRX-4?

I'd consider it if you:

- ✓ Run LED lights on your body
- ✓ Have a light bar or roof lights
- ✓ Have other electronics mounted to the body
- ✓ Remove the body frequently
- ✓ Swap between different bodies
- ✓ Like modifying and upgrading your TRX-4

If your TRX-4 is completely stock and you rarely remove the body, you probably don't need one.

It's not a performance upgrade.

It simply makes **body removal and installation easier.**

And sometimes, those are the little upgrades you appreciate every time you use your RC.

## Common mistakes to avoid

**Only one magnetic half.** You need both the chassis-side mate and the cable-side mate. A single half connects to nothing.

**Magnetic joint mounted too low.** Don't put it near a suspension link or steering rod. Every bit of articulation will tug on the connection. Keep it on the central chassis spine or receiver box area.

**No strain relief on the body side.** When you pick up the shell, the light bar wire adds weight. Zip-tie the body-side lead to the inner body wall so stress doesn't hit the JR pins.

**Confusing it with a battery connector.** This carries BEC-level power for lights and small controllers — not main battery current. Don't substitute it for XT60 or Traxxas ID battery plugs.

## Quick FAQ

**Will this work with the Traxxas TRX-4 LED light kit?**
Yes, if your kit uses a standard 3-pin JR plug — most Traxxas and aftermarket TRX-4 light setups do.

**Does it work on TRX-4 Sport and High Trail?**
Yes. Wire routing may differ slightly with bumpers and grilles, but the connector concept is the same across TRX-4 variants.

**My lights flicker after installing — what's wrong?**
Usually a loose magnetic mate or a JR plug that isn't fully seated. Re-seat both and inspect for bent pins.

**Do I still need body clips?**
Yes, unless you've switched to magnetic body mounts. This cable handles electrical disconnect only — it doesn't hold the body on the chassis.
